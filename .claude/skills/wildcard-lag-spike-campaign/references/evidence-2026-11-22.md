# What one day of instrument data shows (22 Nov 2026, one player)

**Scope, stated once:** one server start (08:56), one day (08:56 to 16:32 Sydney), **one real player** (logged in
at about 14:36 and stayed; in Dungeon Clear runs 14:37-14:59 and 15:22-16:10), 750 bots configured, core `c3f2fd4b2395+` (.114, our patched build). Files read:
`Timing.log` (457 complete records), `perf\perf-2026-11-22_0903.csv` (5,379 rows, every 5 s), `mysql-slow.log` (33
entries), `console-history.txt` (2,522 lines), `Errors.log` (38,273 lines). Every number below was computed from
those files on 2026-11-22 with `scripts/spike_gates.py` or a one-off script; none is from memory or from a
commit message. The sibling skill wildcard-diagnostics-and-tooling ran its own parsers on the same files
(`.claude/skills/wildcard-diagnostics-and-tooling/references/reference-run-2026-11-22.md`) and agrees: 457
complete records, 117 with a player, 77 with a tick of 1 s or more, 22 with 2 s or more. Its "458" is those 457
plus one block cut off at 16:33:10, where the copy was taken mid-write. (Until 2026-11-22 this skill's script
dropped the 14:50:03 record, whose lines arrived out of order, and every count here was one low; the script now
groups a record's lines by their shared timestamp, and the numbers below are from the corrected script. An
early first-pass summary of the day quoted 376 / 97 / 69 / 21; do not use it.)
One day with one player supports "this is what happened that day". It does not support rates,
"always", or a root cause.

Comparing the player-on hours with the empty hours is a trap for every counter except the pattern itself: with
the bots logged in, CPU, memory and MySQL are all higher for ordinary reasons. Compare spike samples with quiet
samples **inside** the player-on time (the `perf-at` "usual" figure does this); there, the busiest single
logical CPU and the process total move, and in the login ramp mysqld does too (A14).

What 6-8 mean together, stated no more strongly than the data allows: on that day something ran **every 20
seconds while a real player was on**; the process's CPU dipped on that beat and the dips were deeper in records
with longer ticks (0.3 to 5.4 s; about two records in three hold a tick of 1 s or more, so "every beat" is not
shown). In the login ramp the largest ticks had one logical CPU at 85-100% while the other threads waited. In
the steady state that one-thread picture is an inference from the correlation, not something a sample shows.

**Read the day as two phases.** Login ramp (14:36 to about 15:00: about 750 bots logging in, memory climbing,
the mysqld pulse of A14 until 14:47, every tick over 3 s). Steady state (15:00 on: the 20.03 s beat, ticks up to
2.9 s, mysqld quiet). A cause proven for one phase is not proven for the other.

What they do **not** show:

- **Which thread.** A world-thread job and a single slow map thread look the same in these counters: in both, the
  other threads wait (`MapMgr::Update` waits for every map before the tick ends).
- **Which code.** Two things in the pinned sources run every 20 s on the world thread and act only when real
  players are on (see `hypotheses.md` H1 and H2). The data cannot tell them apart, and a third is not excluded.
- Whether more players, a city, a battleground or open-world questing change the picture (the player spent 69 of
  the 117 records inside two Dungeon Clear runs).
- How many bots were awake: nothing logs that number ("players online" excludes bots). An instrument gap.
- Whether the smaller excess (window p95 of 95 ms) has the same cause as the long ticks.
- Sub-minute detail from Timing.log itself: its records were 60 s apart and each saw 20-27 s, so the 20 s beat
  was found in the Windows counters, not in Timing.log.

## B. A trap in Timing.log found on this data

The records came 60.07 s apart, which is three beats of 20.03 s. So every record was written at the same point of
the 20 s cycle (14 to 16 s into it, all afternoon). Two consequences:

1. Each window (20-27 s long with a player on) holds one or two beats, so **nearly every record shows a long
   tick** even though ticks that long are about 1 in 500. "66% of minutes have a 1 s spike" (77 of 117) describes the sampling
   as much as the server. Count beats, not minutes.
2. A record's first line ("Update time diff: N ms") is the single tick that crossed the minute mark. It was
   500 ms or more in only 6 of 117 records. Never use that line alone to judge spikes.

## C. Before and after the 19 Nov update, from console-history.txt

What the file is: one line per "Update time diff" console line since the realm began, with start banners between
them and **no timestamps**. Until 21 Nov the server wrote such a line only for a tick over 100 ms, at most once
per 5 minutes; so each old line is "the first slow tick after a 5-minute mark", not a sample of normal ticks.
A `+` after the revision is our patched build; without it, the manager's official build.

| Core revision (build date) | Starts | Lines | Lines with a player on | of those >= 500 ms | Median of all lines | >= 2,000 ms | Largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `90ec1e0a2e58` (23 Oct), official | 20 | 69 | 65 | 12 | 172 ms | 1 | 4,587 ms |
| `a76415e00eba+` (28 Oct), patched | 136 | 586 | 381 | 71 (19%) | 248 ms | 16 | see note |
| `85ecc2122efd+` (8 Nov, release .95), patched | 42 | 1,206 | 929 | 131 (14%) | 289 ms | 9 | 2,970 ms |
| `c3f2fd4b2395` (19 Nov, .114), **official** | 9 | 9 | 2 | 2 | 123 ms | 1 | 2,655 ms |
| `c3f2fd4b2395+` (19 Nov, .114), patched | 13 | 265 | 210 | 156 (74%) | 758 ms | 20 | 4,486 ms |

Note: two revisions carry absurd values (1,110,873 ms and 83,052,825 ms), most likely a clock jump or a
suspended VM; ignore the "largest" there.

Reading it with care:

- **Slow ticks became larger after the 19 Nov step.** With a player on, 14% of the logged slow ticks reached
  500 ms on .95 and 74% on .114; the median logged tick went 289 -> 758 ms. This is the strongest "before/after"
  evidence there is, and it is confounded: bot count (1,250/1,000 then 1,000/750), the console-logging problem
  (present 19-20 Nov), who was on and where all differ, and after 21 Nov the logging rule itself changed (every
  minute, any size), which if anything pulls the .114 median **down**.
- **Multi-second ticks are old.** 4,587 ms on the 23 Oct official build; 9 ticks over 2 s on .95.
- **The official .114 build also produced them, once.** Its only two lines with a player on are 2,655 ms and
  1,451 ms. Two lines from one session are an existence proof that our C++ patches are not required for a 2.6 s
  tick; they say nothing about how often. The settings in force during that session are not known.
- The file cannot give rates per hour (no timestamps, no uptime).

## D. Branches this data closes, and what would reopen them

| Branch | Closed by | Re-check if |
|---|---|---|
| Disk latency (MySQL flush, log writes, antivirus scan, backup) | A9: writes under 1 ms all day, counters never stalled | spikes appear at a different time of day (4 am restart, backup windows), or with more players |
| Paging / memory pressure | A9: never under 5.1 GB free | world server working set passes ~12 GB, or `Available MBytes` drops under 2,000 |
| Whole-VM stall (Proxmox, Windows) | A9: counter rows never more than 6.2 s apart | any `sampler-gap` hint from `perf-at` |
| A slow SQL **statement** on the game loop (both phases) | A10: no entry over 0.5 s after 10:32 | entries appear in `mysql-slow.log` at spike times |
| Waiting on the database / a lock: closed for the **steady state only** | after 15:00 mysqld never passed 26% and no statement was slow. **Open for the login ramp** (A14: mysqld at about one core every 40 s, two `mysql-busy` spikes), even though the seven clearest one-thread samples there had mysqld at 6-9% | any `mysql-busy` hint; `perf-at` showing process CPU falling with **no** busy CPU on many spikes |
| All CPUs busy / too few cores | with the player on: total CPU median 28%, highest sample 52%; processor queue 4 at most | total CPU passes ~70% |
| "It only lags while the bots log in" | A4: flat for two hours | - |
| Console logging inside the tick (a candidate cause; the 20 Nov change f9dc45a) | **Not closed.** Shipped 20 Nov; no after-measurement; `world-console.log` was 2.99 GB on 22 Nov, and the 22 Nov server start (08:53) predates the merges of #229-#231, so the console-log clear/rotation had not run yet; status OPEN. A8 only says a logging stall (a wait) does not look like the seven one-thread samples. See SKILL.md, "Check whether the logging change holds" | `world-console.log` grows by gigabytes a day after the rotation has run |

Not closed: many **fast** synchronous queries in a burst (each under 0.5 s would not be logged): in the login
ramp the mysqld pulse of A14 is exactly what that would look like; in the steady state mysqld at a few percent
leaves little room for it. Also not closed: anything that only happens with 2+ players, in
dungeons, battlegrounds or cities.
